## 1. Discovery: production was never running the code we were tuning

The repo held **two** `best_controller.py` files with byte-identical `params.json`:

```
best_controller.py             (repo root)   -> COPYed into the Docker image, served by the VPS
experiments/best_controller.py              -> the file every parameter sweep actually tuned
```

The root copy had `grep -c repro_energy_abs` = **0** and `relay_age` = **0** — none of the relay /
absolute-energy-gate work existed there. So every leaderboard score of that period
(580.96 / 655.95 / 771.8 / 860.2 / 631.06) was produced by a controller nobody had benchmarked, and
the "improvements" we were measuring could never have reached the grader.

An A/B of the two copies on 8 seeds (identical params) measured them as **behaviourally different —
5/8 seeds diverged**:

```
seed   ROOT(live)   EXP(tuned)   delta
100      5286         4674        -612
200      5454         5320        -134
300      9676         9130        -546

500      3620         3620           0
600      5768         7997       +2229

800      5826         5826           0
```

The root copy also lacked `reset_memory()`, so it carried per-agent state **across episodes inside one

**Fix (committed `540cee2`):** root is the single canonical file; the duplicate is deleted; the

guards single-source + a recorded sha256; `Dockerfile.vps` runs `sha256sum -c` so a build refuses to
ship anything but the reviewed file. The build log confirms the gate: `best_controller.py: OK`.

## 2. Discovery: the harness was nondeterministic, so most tuning signal was noise

Same seed, same controller, same params — **four runs, four answers**: `601.6 / 626.1 / 611.0 / 678.3`.
Three different harnesses disagreed on seed 100: `4674 / 5363 / 5368`.

Isolation procedure that found it:
1. **Constant policy** (zero state) → identical ×3 in one process and across two processes ⇒ the sim
   looked clean.
2. Instrumented the policy's module state: `_MEM/_GC/_AGE/_EPOCH` all cleared at episode start, and
   `_GC`/`_AGE` were empty at the first policy call ⇒ not policy-state leakage.
3. Recorded a **per-tick fingerprint** of the whole world and diffed two runs. First divergence: one
   agent's *position* differed at tick ~1,200 while its energy and age were **identical** — i.e. the
   same input produced a slightly different action.

Root cause: `_get_local_fruits` / `_get_local_objects` (`environment.py:157-207`) return **sets of
entity objects**, and Python hashes objects by identity, so iteration order followed **memory
addresses**. That order flows into (a) the observation list the policy reads and (b) the fruit-eating
loop (`for fruit in reversed(local_fruits)`) — so float summation order differed per episode, and
chaos amplified it. A stateless policy is immune, which is exactly why every earlier determinism test
passed while the real policy did not.

**Fix (committed `74d3ea1`):** sort those lists by position in our local `src/`
(`creature.py` process_objects + hit_edges, `environment.py` fruit loop). After: **462.3278 ×4
in-process and ×4 in a fresh process.** This is a measurement fix in our copy — it changes our
numbers only, never the grader's world.

Three findings: (1) **income collapses after t≈3,000** and goes *negative* after t≈4,000 — the fleet

metabolism. d=`0.1`/tick, biome `energy_drain_rate` is 1.0 everywhere, so metabolism is exactly the
score rate **per agent**.

## 4. Discovery: income is ACCESS-limited, not supply-limited

Direct measurement from the live world (deterministic harness), final 2,000 ticks:

```
seed 100 (4,472 ticks):  standing fruit = 4,518 energy (89.6 fruits) | fleet energy = 1,109 | income +287
                         fruits visible per agent = 0.3 | ticks with NO fruit visible = 576/1998 (29%)
seed 300 (6,828 ticks):  standing fruit = 3,663 energy (75.4 fruits) | fleet energy = 2,173
                         fruits visible per agent = 0.6 | ticks with NO fruit visible = 249/1998 (12%)
```

The world holds **3-4× more food energy than the entire fleet owns**, the fleet captures almost none
of it, and in a sixth to a third of the endgame ticks **no agent can see a single fruit**. The fleet
starves next to food it cannot find — a much better problem than a supply ceiling, because it is a
search problem.

## 8. Methodology rules that earned their place today

1. **Hold out seeds and refuse to believe train-seed wins.** Six hypotheses died this way; shipping
   any of them on the train number would have been a regression.
2. **Resample fitness seeds per generation** during search — a fixed small seed set invites the exact
   overfitting that produced those false wins.
3. **Prove determinism before trusting a ranking** — and prove it with the *real* policy. A constant
   policy passes while the real policy fails.
4. **Localise divergence with a per-tick fingerprint**, not by intuition.
5. **One heavy job at a time, launched in the background.** A foreground tool timeout killed a
   completed first config; the skill warns about this and it still happened.
6. **Measure before and after any infra change** that could touch the live path (latency before/after
   the search; connection count before a redeploy).
7. **Never overwrite the deployed artifact from a search process.** The old `evolve.py` wrote straight
   into `best_controller/params.json`; a crash mid-run could have mutated what the grader scores.
   `evolve_det.py` writes only `evolve_det_best.json`; adoption is a separate explicit step.

## 11. The phase-aware (boom/famine) policy — built, then REFUTED by its own ablations

The target moved to **~1,800 score (~18,000 ticks, 2.2x current)**. The arithmetic for that is
sobering and worth recording, because it explains why thrift cannot get there:

```
to hold one survivor for 12,000 ticks after the famine, metabolism alone (0.1/tick) drains ~1,200
energy -- more than the DEFAULT max_energy cap of 500, and only just under the mutated cap of 1000.
So a survivor must ARRIVE at the famine full, and max_energy (heritable, mutates +/-50%) is what
sets how long it can outlast the food.
```

Built: a boom→famine policy in the canonical controller (all inert by default, proven by re-running
the pre-patch seed to the same `462.3278` on macOS and `7816 ticks` on Linux), consisting of
(a) **sim-time reconstruction** — the state carries NO clock, so time is recovered as
`sim_tick = max(birth_tick + calls_since_birth)` per agent, using `age` (sim-seconds, 1 tick = 0.1 s)
for the initial cohort; validated against ground truth at **max error 1 tick**;
(b) a **time-primary famine ramp** with an optional adaptive food-availability guard;
(c) famine-specific movement and reproduction rules.

Results on Linux (the grader's platform), seed 100:

```
baseline (phase OFF)       7816 ticks
phase on lo=4000 hi=7500   4784     -3032
phase on lo=3000 hi=6000   3988     -3828
phase on lo=5000 hi=9000   6140     -1676
```

The bundle coupled movement + reproduction, so it was ablated (movement held EXACTLY at baseline):

```
A0 baseline                                    7816
A1 spawn-gate only (pop<=3, bank 0.9)          4719
A5 spawn-gate, bank relaxed to 0.5             4719   <- bank NOT binding
A6 spawn-gate, bank removed entirely           4720   <- bank NOT binding
```

Since relaxing and then removing the bank requirement changed nothing, the binding condition is the
**population cap itself**: at tick 4,000+ the fleet holds 10-16 agents, so the gate forbids all
reproduction — and because agents are mortal by age (600-1,200 tick lifetimes), a fleet that stops
reproducing dies out. Both halves of the phase policy therefore failed, and for the same underlying
reason:

* **Cutting movement kills income** — food is ACCESS-limited (75-90 fruits standing while agents
  starve); travel is how it is found, so movement is the income mechanism, not discretionary burn.
  Interpreting "movement = 47% of burn" as waste was the error.
* **Cutting reproduction kills the relay** — mortality is by age, so births are load-bearing.

**Consolidated picture after ~12 experiments:** the fleet is **throughput-limited, not
expenditure-limited**. It needs many mobile foragers and continuous reproduction; every thrift
measure reduces income faster than it reduces cost. The plateau is a ceiling on captured energy per
tick against a production curve that decays as `0.5^(t/300)`, and 18,000 ticks requires a genuine
increase in capture efficiency — which no hypothesis tested so far has achieved, and which the
parameter search may or may not find.

## 12. Search modification worth recording: the horizon cap was limiting discrimination

At horizon 9,000 the fitness saturates: generation 4's winner scored 8,661.3 vs the incumbent's
8,433.3 with per-seed `[7984, 9000, 9000]` — **two of three seeds capped**, so the gain was a LOWER
BOUND and further improvements would be invisible (all capped candidates tie). Fix: horizon raised
to 12,000 for headroom, plus **fruit eaten as a tie-breaker** in the ranking (capped candidates still
differ in what they ate, and that is the income driving survival). The search was resumed from the
generation-4 winner rather than restarted, so no progress was discarded.
